\subsection{Label modes}
GEOID-Flood separates permanent from flooded water, and the two are different tasks. We report three
scores. \emph{All water} (permanent plus flooded, the Sen1Floods11 convention that the
Sen1Floods11-trained models were trained to predict) measures water detection in a single post-event image.
\emph{Flooded water only} scores the same prediction against the flood class, with permanent-water pixels
left out of scoring so that a model trained to find all water is not penalized for finding rivers and lakes;
it measures how well newly inundated water is delineated. GEOID-Flood's own \emph{protocol}
(Section~\ref{sec:methods}) differences a pre- and a post-event prediction and counts permanent water as
non-flood; it is the closest of the three to an operational flood-extent product. A terrain gain on all water
alone would mean better water detection, not better flood mapping, so we report flooded-only and all-water
scores throughout and the protocol for the main comparisons.

\subsection{Overlap and cleaning}
Table~\ref{tab:events} shows how the test events are selected. Of the 9,192 training tiles in the catalog, 254 are flagged invalid or are more than 95\%
nodata (which removes two events entirely, leaving 159 of 161), and 226 of the remaining 8,938 have no
post-event image or no label in the release, leaving 8,712. Sen1Floods11's Sri Lanka (EMSR205) and
Nigeria (EMSR314) events are in GEOID-Flood train; when GEOID-trained models are tested on Sen1Floods11,
we drop those events and Spain.

\subsection{Unseen activations}
\label{sec:unseen}
GEOID-Flood splits by AoI, not by activation: 41 of the 80 test activations also have AoIs in the
training split, and 15 in validation (all but EMSR586 also in training); no test tile has the footprint of
a training or validation tile. For GEOID-trained models we therefore report results on the 38 flood (39
all-water) test events whose activation does not appear in GEOID-Flood train (``unseen activations'').
These models select checkpoints on the validation split, so we repeat every such result on the 37/38 events
absent from training and validation (pre-registered; Table~S14); no conclusion changes. The Sen1Floods11-trained models use no
GEOID-Flood data, so all 77/79 test events are unseen for them.
